Song-Ze Yu · Research

Building models that can hear before they speak.

I study audio-language models as perceptual systems. The goal is not only to generate plausible language about sound, but to understand what a model actually hears, how that knowledge is represented, and how a musician can work with it.

Research questions

Three connected problems.

Musical perception

Can a general audio-language model identify pitch, rhythm, and structure reliably when the sound source, duration, notation, or acoustic context changes?

Useful representation

How can symbolic score information and time-aligned performance features coexist in a representation that a language model can inspect and reason over?

Human-led interaction

How can models respond to a musician's intent without flattening the choices that make a performance personal, expressive, and worth revising?

Preprint · arXiv:2605.26176

PitchBench: Measuring Pitch Hearing in Audio-Language Models

Milan Liessens Dujardin, Song-Ze Yu, Craver Corbyn Thomas-Smith, David M. Chan, Karina Nguyen

PitchBench introduces 28 controlled experiments covering absolute and relative pitch, sequences, chords, sound sources, time stretching, background noise, and notation formats. Across frontier models, pitch perception remains unreliable and varies sharply with the acoustic and response conditions.

InstructFX2FX: A Multi-turn Text-to-Preset Demo for Iterative Audio Effect Refinement

Song-Ze Yu, Milan Liessens Dujardin, Yuxuan Cai, Wantong Zhang

InstructFX2FX treats audio production as a stateful interaction. An LLM plans an effect chain and initial parameters, while CLAP-guided optimization refines the existing sound across successive instructions such as “make it warmer” or “less harsh.”
