Profile

An engineer, a musician.

I'm a Computer Science student at UC Berkeley (exchange from National Tsing Hua University), working on machine learning for music and audio. My long-term goal is to build audio-language models for music — the way vision-language models reshaped how we reason about images, I believe similar foundations are needed for sound.

Today, my research lives at the intersection of two labs: Berkeley AI Research (BAIR) under Prof. Trevor Darrell, and the Center for New Music & Audio Technologies (CNMAT) under Prof. Carmine-Emanuele Cella. Two papers are currently under review at ISMIR 2026 and NeurIPS 2026 Datasets & Benchmarks.

Before research, I was a child piano prodigy — first prize at my first competition, then 24 solo recitals and more than 70 TV appearances across Greater China between 2009 and 2016, including three on-stage moments with Jay Chou. I still play, write, and produce. That dual lens of engineer and musician is the through-line of everything I build.

Publications & Research

Research

My research focuses on audio-language models, music information retrieval, and the perceptual grounding of machine listening — combining the rigor of deep learning with the ear of a working musician.

Under Review — 2026

MUNO-LM: Unifying Score and Performance for Audio-Language Modeling Under Review

Song-Ze Yu, Carmine-Emanuele Cella, et al.

ISMIR 2026 · Center for New Music & Audio Technologies (CNMAT), UC Berkeley

A unified audio-language framework that bridges symbolic score and recorded performance — letting one model reason across notation and sound. Built at CNMAT under Prof. Carmine-Emanuele Cella.

PitchBench: Measuring Pitch Hearing in Audio-Language Models Under Review

Song-Ze Yu, David M. Chan, Trevor Darrell, et al.

NeurIPS 2026 — Datasets & Benchmarks Track · BAIR Lab, UC Berkeley

A perceptually-grounded benchmark that probes whether audio-language models can actually hear pitch — covering interval, register, transposition, and contour tasks across musical and non-musical sounds.

In Preparation — planned 2026

Anamnesis: An Open-Source Platform for Large-Scale Backstory-Conditioned Survey Simulation EMNLP Demo · Planned

Song-Ze Yu, Joseph Suh, Yutong Bai, Serina Chang, David M. Chan.

EMNLP 2026 — Demo Track · BAIR Lab, UC Berkeley

A scalable platform for demographically-controlled multimodal survey simulation and virtual population studies. Achieved closer alignment to ground-truth population distributions than individual human respondents on ATP surveys. Originally submitted to ACL 2026 Demo; being extended for EMNLP 2026 Demo.

InstructFX2FX: Text-to-Preset Generation with LLM Initialization and Human-in-the-Loop Gradient Refinement DAFx · Planned

Song-Ze Yu, Carmine-Emanuele Cella.

DAFx 2026 · CNMAT Lab, UC Berkeley

A hybrid system pairing LLM semantic priors with gradient-based optimization. Parses natural-language feedback (“less bright”, “more warm”) into directional updates in CLAP embedding space, enabling iterative, controllable sound design.

Earlier Work — 2025

From Sound to Setting: AI-Based EQ Parameter Prediction for Piano Tone Replication Preprint

Song-Ze Yu, Yi-Wen Liu.

arXiv 2509.24404 · AHG Music Lab, National Tsing Hua University

Built a ReaScript-generated dataset of piano recordings with varied multi-band EQ parameters and trained a supervised neural network to predict EQ settings directly from reference audio DSP features (MSE 0.0216). The model became the core of VTR-SmartEQ, a working JUCE plugin.

A life in music.

I learned piano by ear. My first competition — a national piano contest in Taiwan — I won first place without a teacher. What followed, between 2009 and 2016, was a child-star decade: more than 70 TV appearances, 24 solo piano recitals across mainland China and Taiwan, three on-stage moments with Jay Chou, and a featured guest spot at Yan Jue's Taipei Arena concert.

I never stopped writing. I have 20+ original songs, and my single “失電陣線聯盟” crossed 14.4K plays on Instagram. In college I played keys, lead vocal, cajón, and electric guitar across several bands — whatever the song needed, I'd fill in. If it's keyboard, I don't need rehearsal: one listen and I can improvise the part on stage.
